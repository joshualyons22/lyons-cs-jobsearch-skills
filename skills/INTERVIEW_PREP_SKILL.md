## INTERVIEW APPROACH

**Format preference:** SAR (Situation, Action, Result) — not STAR. The Task step is usually obvious and wastes time better spent on Action and Result.

**Delivery principle:** Lead with the result when possible (inverted SAR for senior roles). Be specific about your role vs. the team's role. No story longer than 2 minutes spoken / 200 words written.

**Tone:** Confident, specific, human. Never rehearsed-sounding. The goal is to sound like someone telling a true story, not reciting a script.

**Interview formats expected:**
- Behavioral (SAR) — most common
- Panel interviews
- Possible presentation or project component

## COMMON INTERVIEW QUESTIONS — PREP NOTES

### "Walk me through your background."
[Write your opening line — the through-line that frames your career before going role by role. Lead with your defining theme, not your job history.]

Format: Open with the theme → Briefly cover each role (30 seconds each, metrics-first)

### "Why are you leaving / why are you available?"
[Write your answer. Keep it factual, forward-looking, and free of bitterness. If laid off, state the facts clearly. If leaving by choice, frame around the opportunity, not what you're leaving.]

Never: Elaborate on internal company politics, express bitterness, speculate about decisions.

### "What's your approach to at-risk accounts?"
[Write your 3-4 sentence answer. Lead with a result, then describe your philosophy and approach briefly before directing to your SAR story.]

### "How do you drive expansion?"
[Write your answer — which story(ies) to use and how to connect them to your philosophy about expansion.]

### "How do you partner with Sales?"

## QUESTIONS TO ASK INTERVIEWERS

*These signal strategic thinking, not just job-seeking. Customize for each company.*

2. "Where are the biggest gaps in the current CS motion — retention, expansion, onboarding, something else?"

4. "What does success look like in the first 90 days for this role?"
5. "How is the CS team using AI tools today — and is there appetite for CSMs who want to build on top of that?"
6. "What's the biggest challenge the team is dealing with right now that this hire would help address?"

## PANEL INTERVIEW NOTES

When multiple interviewers are present:
- Make initial eye contact with whoever asked the question, then sweep the room during the answer
- Don't ignore quieter panel members — they often have the most influence
- If asked the same question twice by different people, give the same core answer but vary the example if possible
- At the end, ask a question directed at the panel collectively: "Is there anything any of you wanted to go deeper on that we didn't get to?"

## PRESENTATION / PROJECT PREP

If asked to prepare a 30/60/90 day plan or a CS strategy presentation:

**Structure:**
- Day 1-30: Listen, learn, map (customers, internal relationships, data, existing processes)
- Day 31-60: Identify top 3 priorities; begin executing on the most urgent
- Day 61-90: Show early results; propose longer-term improvements with data

**Your differentiators to weave in:**
- At-risk account identification and recovery methodology
- Stakeholder alignment approach (validate before proposing)
- AI-powered workflow audit (what can be automated; what should stay human)
- Pipeline contribution framework (CS as pipeline contributor, not just retention)
