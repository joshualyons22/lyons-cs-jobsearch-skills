## PHASE 1: INTELLIGENCE GATHERING (Before First Customer Contact)

### What to do before the first conversation:
- Review all available usage data to validate what you've been told about the account's status
- Interview the outgoing CSM(s) thoroughly — not just about the problems, but about the backstory and the "why" behind each issue
- Read all historical communications, support tickets, and internal notes
- Identify who the key stakeholders are on both sides and their relationships with each other
- Map what was promised vs. what was delivered — be specific

### Why this matters:
Walking into the first customer conversation already knowing their challenges signals that you've done your homework. It removes the burden from the customer of having to catch you up, and it's the first trust-building moment — before you've even addressed a single problem.

### Red flags that signal an account may be unrecoverable:
- Customer leadership has already shifted attention to a competitor and is actively evaluating alternatives
- Budget has been reallocated away from your product in the next cycle
- No internal champion remains — everyone who believed in the product has left or disengaged
- The customer has explicitly stated they will not renew regardless of intervention

### Important nuance:
An account that appears to be a lost cause can still be turned — if the right resource shows up and takes the time to understand what it would take to keep the business. Don't write off an account until you've had the first real conversation.

## PHASE 2: THE FIRST CONVERSATION

### Principles:
- **Never stoke existing fires.** Don't open with defense, explanation, or apology on behalf of your company.
- **Don't throw anyone under the bus** — not previous CSMs, not Sales, not Product. Acknowledge that things came up short without assigning blame.
- **Lead with awareness, not agenda.** Demonstrate that you understand their situation before asking anything of them.
- **Ask what you might not know.** After showing your understanding, ask if there are details you're missing.

### Opening approach:
1. Communicate what you understand the challenges to be — specifically, by name
2. Validate their frustrations directly and sincerely
3. Ask if there's anything you haven't covered that you should know
4. Listen more than you talk — this conversation is about understanding, not solving
5. Do not make commitments you can't keep in this first conversation

### What NOT to do:
- Don't present a solution plan in the first conversation — you haven't earned the right yet
- Don't minimize their frustrations or pivot too quickly to "here's what we're going to do"
- Don't speak negatively about any internal colleague, previous CSM, or your own product
- Don't make promises to get through the meeting

## PHASE 3: PRIORITIZATION FRAMEWORK

### How to prioritize issues:
Not all problems are equal. Prioritize by impact — what will have the greatest effect on removing a negative or delivering a required positive outcome for the customer.

**Two categories to balance:**

**Quick wins** — Issues that are relatively easy to address and create immediate positive momentum. Their value isn't in their magnitude — it's in demonstrating that things are changing and that you follow through.

**Strategic wins** — The harder, more significant issues that will ultimately determine whether the customer stays. These take longer but are what the relationship actually hinges on.

**Practical prioritization test:**
- Will resolving this remove a significant source of frustration or distrust?
- Will resolving this deliver something the customer has specifically said they need?
- Is this something we can actually fix, or are we raising false hope?
- If we fix this, does it open the door to the next conversation?

## PHASE 4: EXECUTION AND TRUST BUILDING

### Communication principles:
- **Over-communicate** — especially when the answer is "no" or "not yet." Silence breeds distrust.
- **Set realistic expectations and never over-promise.** One broken commitment undoes weeks of progress.
- **Follow through on everything you commit to**, no matter how small. Consistency is the currency of trust.
- **Keep the customer informed of wins** — even small ones. Name the progress explicitly so they feel it.

## PHASE 5: INTERNAL STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT

### Principles:
- **Collaboration over authority.** You often have no formal authority over Sales, Product, or Engineering. Influence comes from being someone people want to work with.
- **Make their jobs easier.** Take on whatever is outside other stakeholders' core responsibilities.
- **Set clear expectations internally** — what needs to happen, by when, and why it matters.
- **Follow up without making people defensive.**

### Practical execution:
- Own all scheduling for cross-functional meetings
- Handle most customer communications directly (keeping internal stakeholders informed but not burdened)
- Coordinate information flow between internal teams and between internal teams and the customer
- Brief internal stakeholders before customer conversations so everyone shows up aligned

---

## PHASE 6: KNOWING WHEN YOU'VE WON

Recovery is not a single pivot point. An account is truly recovered — not just stabilized — when all of the following are true:

1. **Your primary contact is your in-house champion again** — they're advocating for you internally, not just tolerating you
2. **Active collaboration is happening** — they're sharing internal artifacts, bringing you into executive meetings, co-developing plans with you
3. **Renewal is confirmed and budgeted** — not just a verbal signal, but evidence that the budget has been allocated

Until all three are true, the account is stabilized but not recovered. Stabilized is good; recovered is the goal.

## INTERVIEW APPLICATION

### Key themes to hit in turnaround questions:

**"Walk me through how you approach at-risk accounts"**
Lead with the philosophy. Then walk through the phases: intelligence gathering before first contact, the first conversation (listen, validate, don't promise), prioritization (quick wins + strategic wins), execution (over-communicate, follow through, never over-promise), internal alignment (make everyone's job easier), and the recovery markers.

**"Tell me about a time you saved a customer relationship"**
Use your Story 1 from INTERVIEW_PREP_SKILL. Key moments to hit:
- Did your homework before first call
- Led with validation, not solutions
- Prioritized their most impactful issues, not the easiest ones
- Over-communicated including when the answer was no
- The trust inflection point
- Quantified result

**"What's your approach to a customer who's already decided to leave?"**
Acknowledge that some accounts are genuinely unrecoverable. But don't write off an account until you've had the first real conversation with the right resource.

**"How do you manage internal stakeholders during a difficult account situation?"**
Hit the "make their jobs easier" principle. Own the scheduling, communications, and coordination. Follow up without making people defensive.
