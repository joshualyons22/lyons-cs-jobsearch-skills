## HOW TO USE THIS TEMPLATE

**Step 1 — JD Analysis (5 min):**
Read the job posting and identify:
- The 2-3 most specific phrases that describe what the role is really about
- The primary pain point they're hiring to solve (churn? expansion? onboarding? all three?)
- Any specific products, platforms, or methodologies they mention
- Company-specific context (stage, industry, what makes them distinctive)

**Step 2 — Fill in the brackets:**
Every [BRACKETED] field is a deliberate insertion point. Don't skip them — they're what make the letter feel written for this role, not recycled.

**Step 3 — Read it aloud:**
If any sentence sounds like it was written by a resume writer or a chatbot, rewrite it. The test: would you say this in an interview?

**Step 4 — Generate PDF:**
Ask Claude: "Generate the cover letter PDF using the IC template with these fields filled in: [paste your filled fields]"

## TAILORING NOTES BY ROLE TYPE

**If the role emphasizes churn/retention:**
- Lead Para 2 with your largest ARR save or longest retention streak
- Add your philosophy about at-risk accounts specifically

**If the role emphasizes expansion/NRR:**
- Emphasize ARR growth % and any net-new pipeline contribution
- Add: "I've always seen expansion as a byproduct of trust — not a separate motion."

**If the role emphasizes onboarding/time-to-value:**
- Add your onboarding improvement metrics (TTFV reduction, CSAT improvement)

**If the role emphasizes AI/innovation:**
- Expand the builder paragraph with specific time savings
- Move the builder paragraph before the retention paragraph

**If the role is at a security/compliance company:**
- Add any compliance-adjacent background you have (certifications, compliance-driven customer experience, regulated industry background)

**If the role is at an early-stage company:**
- Add your builder/founding CSM experience: "I've built CS from scratch before — I know what it takes to build something that works before you have the infrastructure to make it easy."

## CRITICAL NARRATIVE RULES

**What to ALWAYS do:**
- Lead with what you do (your turnaround/builder/relationship theme)
- Use specific, earned language (numbers, product names, methodologies)
- Be honest about domain expertise — don't overclaim

**What to NEVER do:**
- Don't force industry specifics into sentences where they don't belong
- Don't claim expertise you haven't earned
- Don't use a saccharine or generic closing
- Don't open with your job title and where you saw the posting

**Departure context:**
One sentence maximum. Factual, non-defensive, forward-looking.

---

**These elements should stay consistent across all IC applications:**
- The opening structure (JD mirror → identity statement)
- Your core retention/save narrative with specific metrics
- The AI/builder paragraph (if applicable) — this is your differentiator; don't bury it
- The departure sentence — one line, factual, no elaboration
- The closing ask — direct, no filler
